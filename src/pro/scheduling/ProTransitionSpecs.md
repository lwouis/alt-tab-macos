# Pro trial journey

The 14-day trial introduces useful features without adding an independent reminder budget for each feature. Product days below are one-based; `daysSinceTrialStart` is zero-based. The late reminder explicitly starts after 63 complete elapsed days (product day 64). New trial starts follow the first-usable milestone in `LicenseManagerSpecs.md`; saved dates, elapsed-day semantics, license precedence, signature and license Keychain accounts remain unchanged. Existing checkout attribution codes are retained; the late-reminder experiment adds `a9` and an optional arm.

## Inventory and ownership

- `ProTransitionManager` coordinates license transitions, one last Pro session, dismissal and prompt actions. `ProTransitionState` persists eligibility, opt-out and the pre-downgrade preferences. `ProTransitionScheduler` owns the single timed work item: one wall-clock GCD deadline for the Welcome letter, the start of day 13 or the trial's end, never a repeating timer. The target date is persisted, so a relaunch fires an overdue one and arms the next. Opt-out suppresses promotional deadlines while preserving trial expiration.
- `SearchDiscoveryHint` owns the nonactivating switcher child panel and the shared Search/extra-shortcut history. `SearchDiscoveryPolicy` decides eligibility, cooldown and placement.
- `AnchoredPopover` waits for a usable menu icon and falls back below the menu bar if it is hidden or unavailable. It acknowledges actual presentation with `didShow`, cancels stale work and releases content on close.
- History loading and anchor layout recheck the current session, competing UI, fullscreen, license, opt-out and daily message budget before showing proactive content. A pending popover excludes itself from the competing-UI check. User-requested gate feedback bypasses marketing limits, but still requires an available session without onboarding. The small blocked-feature popover is allowed during switching; full windows still wait until the switcher is inactive. Lock, sleep, a Space change, a new switcher session or opening another window cancels outstanding history loads; returning to an eligible context does not revive them.
- Onboarding continues to explain permissions and the configured shortcut, then opens the complete Settings window. Its pending completion occupies the prompt slot. No first-launch style chooser is added.
- `LicenseManager` remains the entitlement authority. Purchase cancels deferred marketing, timed work, and existing Pro prompts. After launch, license changes re-arm the same single deadline, including a previously unstarted trial begun by deactivation. Before launch completes, scheduling waits for onboarding to claim its slot. Successful new activation has a separate callback from revalidation.

## Welcome and setup

Unresolved first setup has no trial clock and no promotional scheduling. Once usable, fresh installs announce the trial in the existing onboarding completion card; the fallback/existing-install route uses the welcome letter, a popover under the menu bar icon like the other prompts. Both say “Pro is included until [localized date]. After that, window switching and thumbnails stay free. Search, extra styles, Auto Size, and additional shortcuts require Pro.” The letter's Continue button, Escape or a click outside only dismisses it; on a first launch, Settings opens once it has closed. Both routes record Welcome as seen only after actual presentation; the letter also acknowledges a Continue click that precedes the appearance notification. Cancellation while its anchor is pending consumes nothing. The scheduler still attempts Welcome at most once per lifetime; an interrupted letter can be retried on a later launch, with no immediate reschedule loop. The letter rechecks that the trial is active when its anchor becomes ready. Neither route restarts the clock. Resuming shortcut onboarding recomputes the card from the current license state/end date; an expired or activated trial gets no new-trial card or letter. Existing onboarding navigation and prompt occupancy are unchanged.

## Proactive surfaces

| Surface | Trigger | Missed opportunity |
|---|---|---|

## Late-reminder experiment

Eligibility uses the existing trial start and requires `.trialExpired`, at least `63 * 86400` elapsed seconds, no shared `userOptedOut`, and at least `7 * 86400` seconds since the last Pro message. Missing message history imposes no cooldown. A future message date blocks. The experiment becomes available when the version is installed, at any hour. Pro, unresolved setup, active trials and version-expired purchased licenses are excluded.

Louis approved two presentation opportunities. On the first usable launch after a version upgrade, the reminder is queued for the next main-runloop turn. `PreferencesMigrations` compares the previously saved `preferencesVersion` with the current version before overwriting it. A strictly increasing numeric version identifies an upgrade, including Sparkle, automatic updates and manual bundle replacement. Fresh installs, ordinary relaunches, downgrades and development version placeholders do not count. This works for upgrades from releases that predate the reminder. The startup opportunity is attempted once per launch, once permissions and onboarding have been evaluated and the prompt host exists.

After the user selects a window in AltTab, the reminder can also appear one second after dismissal, behind any queued free-pass or expiry announcement. `App.focusSelectedWindow` signals the selected window's focus request after initiating the visible dismissal and focus work. Escape, an empty selection and dismissals that cannot request window focus leave this opportunity unused. Merely opening or cycling the switcher does not queue the reminder. A switcher use or an update counts as today's activity for eligibility and checkout attribution.

Users who update before reaching 63 days become eligible on a later confirmed window selection. Users already past the threshold can receive the startup opportunity immediately. The coordinator requests this reminder only from these two events. The single scheduler handles Welcome and trial deadlines. All session, fullscreen, competing-UI, onboarding, recovery and cancellation safeguards apply through deferred dispatch, history loading and anchor placement. An interrupted or blocked attempt waits for a later qualifying event. Existing saved reminder deadlines are cleared without generating a late reminder.

Assignment is the high bit of the first SHA-256 digest byte of UTF-8 `AltTab.late-reminder.2026-12-07.v1`, a NUL byte, then the existing machine fingerprint unchanged: 0 → treatment (`t`), 1 → control (`c`). The date-shaped salt is a permanent experiment identifier and has no scheduling role. The fingerprint and hash stay local; only the arm is persisted in the license defaults suite. Fingerprint acquisition uses the existing local source and Keychain fallback off the main thread, adds no network call, and does not alter license identity. Concurrent requests share one acquisition. The saved arm is never redrawn, including after a relaunch or a fallback change.

Both arms pass the same availability and history-loading checks. Control creates no popover and records only its local would-have-seen opportunity; it does not record a Pro message. Treatment consumes the opportunity only when the popover acknowledges visible presentation (including acknowledgement from a button before the animation finishes). Cancellation before that point consumes nothing. `lateReminder.opportunityAt` permanently prevents another opportunity in either arm. Dismissal, including **Not now**, has no follow-up. **Don't mention again** writes the same permanent refusal as Day 35. Purchase cancels the pending or visible reminder through the shared host.

The treatment uses the existing small anchored-popover format, including the hidden-icon fallback, without taking keyboard focus. Escape and outside clicks dismiss it even while another app is active, with dismissal monitors limited to its visible lifetime; a hidden menu icon does not disable these exits. Title: **AltTab Pro is still here**. Body: **You've used AltTab {N} times over the last {M} months.** followed by **Search, extra shortcuts and App Icons are a one-time purchase away.** Buttons: **Get Pro**, **Not now**; separate refusal link: **Don't mention again**. All strings are in the usual 21-language catalogs.

Louis approved contextual usage instead of “window switches since you installed AltTab”, then requested nearest-month rounding. `M` is elapsed time since trial start divided by 30 days, rounded to the nearest integer (half rounds up), bounded to 2–12. Thus 59 or 63 days → 2 months, 75 or 76 → 3, 180 → 6, 210 → 7, and older histories cap at 12. `N` counts recorded shortcut uses in the corresponding last `M * 30` days, inclusively through now; future events are excluded. The 360-day cap fits the existing 365-day retained history. Counts describe shortcut uses, not completed focus changes or a lifetime total. Malformed history omits the count sentence; a slow read cancels this attempt after the existing two-second deadline, allowing a later opportunity. No data is fabricated or recovered from deleted history.

`CheckoutOrigin.lateReminder` is **a9**, permanently reserved and never reused; retired **a5** stays retired. All Get Pro entry points use the central checkout helper. An eligible Mac adds `arm=t` or `arm=c` alongside the existing `via`. Eligibility can enroll a Mac before the reminder at any hour. Enrollment is saved as `lateReminder.enrolledAt`; once enrolled, later Pro messages do not remove attribution, otherwise the reminder's own message would strip its purchase button's arm. Trial age, free status, today's activity and permanent refusal still gate attribution. Before enrollment the seven-day cooldown also applies. `lateReminder.lastUseAt` stores the day of switcher use or an update. An uncached assignment may delay checkout for at most one second, then checkout opens once without an arm or new enrollment. A late result still caches the arm for later requests but cannot reopen or retroactively enroll that checkout. Cached or ineligible requests open immediately without a timeout; eligibility is rechecked before attribution. These local flags, the arm and the existing message timestamp add no telemetry. Checkout sends only `via` and the optional arm, never a fingerprint, hash, email or new identifier.

Website work is separate and not included in this app change: before releasing the app, `frontend/_includes/js/checkout-origin.js` must accept `a9` and carry an `arm` whose value is exactly `t` or `c` into Paddle custom data, preserving it like `via`. Shipping this app alone does not implement website attribution.

`LateReminderTests` covers the 62/63-day and seven-day boundaries, activity at every hour and across time zones, update readiness, ordinary relaunch suppression, confirmed selection versus cancellation, old-deadline cleanup, older-install catch-up, opt-out, stable hash vectors and persistence, silent control, visible acknowledgement, button actions, all checkout origins, attribution exclusion, month rounding and matching history bounds. `PreferencesMigrationsTests.testUpdateDetectionRequiresAnExistingOlderRelease` pins upgrade detection. Native anchor display is substituted. Actual updater installation, macOS focus, animation, menu-bar placement, VoiceOver and multi-display behavior need interactive QA.

The seven-day intent window is a product policy, not a measured conversion result. Only the last attempted feature and its date are stored locally. Trial counters never establish recent intent. Settings gates and runtime gates both count as attempts; they never increment successful use statistics.

A repeated summon within the dismissal delay cancels the timer until the switcher is dismissed. Rebuilding the same session for another shortcut does not create a second opportunity. Display sleep or leaving the login session cancel pending presentations. A Space change cancels them too, except during the delay after dismissal: focusing a window on another Space is the switch the user asked for, and the prompt follows them there. These observers exist from launch, not from the first prompt. Locked/inactive sessions, fullscreen, onboarding, modal UI, an open menu, another Pro window/popover, and the active switcher block proactive presentation. Anchor readiness is checked again before presenting, including when the status item is absent. Welcome attempts are bounded to one per scheduler lifetime, preventing an immediate reschedule loop if presentation is blocked.

Discovery is limited to the trial; purchase stops both discovery and commercial solicitations. A feature's first actual use permanently stops that feature's discovery. Closing a discovery tip disables both tips through the existing preference. Existing Search exposures, cooldown and refusal migrate without a fresh budget. No Auto tip is scheduled.

## Day 4 interaction

Three existing native style illustrations and three blue buttons. The question uses the macOS emphasized Title 3 metrics (15 pt, semibold), independently of button emphasis. Blue styling is independent of default-button behavior. No Return key equivalent. Escape dismisses without changing preferences. A click selects and closes; Keep Thumbnails writes nothing. The first shortcut's explicit style override is updated together with the global style so the demonstrated choice is visible on the next summon.

## Expiration and blocked gestures

The license state follows the clock only when refreshed, so an app left running past the end of the trial expires it itself: the single timer also wakes at the trial's end, and a switcher dismissal refreshes an outlived trial state once the focus is done. Neither runs while the switcher is open. Timers use wall time, so a Mac asleep at the scheduled moment fires on wake instead of hours later.

Preference downgrade and the existing one-session free pass are retained. The old proactive-expiration flag is treated as an already delivered announcement for migration. Both flags are set when the unified announcement appears, leaving an interrupted history load eligible for a later dismissal. The Day 4 retry stays armed through anchor layout and clears only on acknowledged presentation. Every subsequent newly blocked attempt receives feedback, including after opt-out. Anchored Pro popovers, an open gate included, are closed before a new gate is presented, so the gate always names the latest blocked feature.

After the one-time expiry announcement, a blocked gesture immediately requests the independent menu-bar popover, with the attempted feature's title and Maybe later / Get Pro. It does not dismiss the switcher, change its selected window, cancel the held shortcut, or queue a response for one second after dismissal. The anchor's existing main-queue layout pass presents it; no synchronous native show is added to the gesture handler. The full first announcement retains its dismissal delay.

The gate remains a native menu-bar popover, never a switcher child or inline message. Its application-defined lifetime keeps it available through cycling, modifier release, focusing the chosen app, and later summons. It does not explicitly activate AltTab or make its window key: a delayed anchor must not steal focus back from the window just selected. Escape or a click outside dismisses it, including while another app is active or while AltTab still discards keys after a switcher dismissal; clicks inside keep its buttons usable. Escape still reaches the switcher's shortcut handling, including when its absorption tap intercepts the key. Dismissal monitors exist only while the gate is shown. It has no automatic timeout. Maybe later dismisses only this response; another blocked attempt can show a fresh one. Get Pro closes it and opens the existing attributed checkout. Purchase buttons reject Return and keypad Enter, including when the button has keyboard focus.

Only an already visible gate survives a new summon. A pending anchor request is cancelled on re-summon, opening another secondary window, lock, sleep, leaving the login session, or Space change. Visible gates close on lock, sleep, leaving the login session, Space change, purchase, replacement by a newer gate, or their explicit actions. Space changes retain the current safety policy even when switching to a window on another Space. A gate replaces both visible and pending popovers and cancels older window-history requests, so only the newest attempted feature can appear. An already visible expiry or final-reminder window prevents an additional gate. Proactive Day 4 / Day 35 rules, the one-session free pass, entitlement and the one-time expiry announcement remain unchanged.

## Shared feature presentation

Expiration, Day 35 and Settings use `ProJourneyView`. Its four tabs remain explorable. An expiration announcement brought by a blocked attempt opens on that feature's tab; Day 35 opens on the feature of the recent attempt that made it eligible. Otherwise the deterministic priority is: an adopted Pro style; repeated Search or extra-shortcut actions (at least two, higher count wins, Search breaks ties); Auto configured as active; a single Search or extra-shortcut action (Search breaks ties); generic Auto. An adopted style is a deliberate configuration; Auto can be the default, so repeated actions outrank it. Two actions is a compact ranking threshold, not evidence of successful searches or time saved. A single exploratory Search cannot displace an adopted style, repeated shortcuts, or active Auto. Expiration and Day 35 are windows that ask for a choice: no close or minimize button or shortcut, and Escape or a click elsewhere leaves them open. Maybe later (and Day 35's opt-out) closes them; Get Pro opens checkout and leaves them open. Away from the menu bar icon, their status line names AltTab (“Your AltTab Pro trial has ended”). Both read the history before they appear, so they open on their final tab and height. Settings only changes its automatic selection while hidden; late history cannot move a visible tab, and an explicit tab selection always survives refresh. Day 35 reuses the expiration window's layout, rabbit included; after a trial, its bubble says it saw the user reach for AltTab Pro this week (naming the app, as the window is away from its menu bar icon) and that Pro is still there when needed.

- A nonempty Search query is one started search per switcher session. It is never described as a found window or time saved.
- Extra shortcut use is aggregated across extra slots. Blocked attempts are excluded.
- Counts include timestamps in `[trialStart, trialStart + 14 days)`. Missing, malformed and aged-out history are unknown; unknown counts are hidden. Known expiration counts are archived locally so pruning does not gradually lower them.
- A tried and abandoned style is not adopted. The pre-lock effective style of the last-used trial shortcut, including its explicit override, drives the comparison. Existing downgraded installs without reliable evidence get a generic feature example.
- Styles show the adopted view on the left, Thumbnails on the right, with headings above both images. No duplicate adoption caption appears below them.
- Auto uses the same illustrative windows at two sizes, without scrolling. “During your trial” appears only when Auto was captured as active; otherwise the caption says “With Pro”. The drawing is illustrative, not a simulation of the user's screen dimensions.
- Search, Styles, Shortcuts and Auto Size use self-contained English SVG comparisons in `resources/svg-illustrations`. A build-time compiler maps those sources to shared native drawing instructions and separate JPEGs. The native drawer supplies existing localized strings and evidence-dependent heading overrides; the artwork owns text layout. The app overlays a native Pro badge.
- The Shortcuts comparison is an illustrative example (`ProJourneyViewSpecs.md`). Discovery tips are the place that reads the user's configured keys.
- Beneath the illustration, real usage sits at the leading edge in readable system body text, with no explanatory sentence. Only the measured count is emphasized; missing usage leaves no placeholder.
- Shared localized “One-time purchase” copy appears under Get Pro in Settings, and before the buttons in the hard-gate popover. The expiration and Day 35 windows don't show it. It doesn't mention subscriptions. It wraps in content columns. The direct-checkout menubar item doesn't show it. No prices or checkout destinations change. The Settings sidebar opens Settings, so it does not duplicate these terms.
- In the expiration and Day 35 windows, Maybe later then blue Get Pro sit at the trailing edge. Day 35's persistent opt-out, Don't mention again, sits alone at the leading edge, level with them. Settings has no dismiss button, and keeps license entry and the existing recovery portal.

## Activation and feedback

Only a successful activation transitioning into Pro celebrates. Revalidation, app restart, failed activation and repeated activation while already licensed do not. The confirmation is a popover under the menu bar icon, with the existing green check and confetti, with Reduce Motion respected. A centered heading and unlimited-access subtitle precede one grouped list of four features, each with a green check, benefit and separator. Its single button reads “Enjoy!” and closes it. It never claims all preferences were changed. Feedback uses the same isolated rabbit and speech bubble as discovery. Submission behavior and drafts are unchanged.
